Evaluation of Multilayer Designs for Stable Wearable Biosensors 
Joel Whaling1, Aarav Patel1,2, Mandy Hung1,2, Yuancheng Fang1,2, Leona Cai1,2, Zenan Li1,2, Advik Raja1,2, Nathan Xu1,2, Linda Shi1


1Institute of Engineering in Medicine, University of California San Diego, USA; 
2High school students participating in IEM OPALS


*Corresponding author, E-mail:zshi@ucsd.edu
Abstract. 
Wearable biosensors provide continuous monitoring but suffer from signal drift correlated to stress from skin movement. This study evaluated whether compliant single-buffer or graded two-buffer layers improve mechanical stability over bufferless designs.


Complementary 2D (ANSYS APDL) and 3D (Fusion 360 to ANSYS) finite element models were analyzed. 2D models included skin (2.00 mm, v = 0.49), PDMS substrate (0.40 mm, v = 0.49), gold sensor (0.02 mm, v = 0.34), and encapsulation (0.10 mm). Design 1 was bufferless; Design 2 added a soft buffer (E = 0.10 MPa); Design 3 used soft (0.10 mm, 0.10 MPa) and medium (0.10 mm, 0.50 MPa) buffers. A 1.00 mm skin displacement was applied to evaluate maximum displacement (DMX) and von Mises stress (SEQV). 3D models used a 1 mm Neo-Hookean PDMS substrate tested under stretching, compression, and bending with no buffer, one 0.5 mm soft buffer, or two 0.25 mm graded buffers.


In 2D tests, displacement decreased from 0.001816 mm (bufferless) to 0.001080 mm (single buffer, ~40.5% lower) and 0.001285 mm (graded, ~29.2% lower). However, maximum SEQV  increased from 2.39992 MPa (bufferless) to 3.16 MPa (single) and 3.05206 MPa (graded)—a ~3.4% reduction from single to graded buffers. In 3D stretching, average stress fell from 7.82 MPa (bufferless) to 6.50 MPa (single) and 6.31 MPa (graded), dropping ~17% and ~19%. Bending (6.10, 6.11, 6.02 MPa) and compression (1.19, 1.28, 1.21 MPa) showed minimal buffer dependence.


Buffers reduced 2D deformation but increased peak stress versus bufferless setups. Graded designs outperformed single buffers and optimized 3D stretching relief. Thus, buffer architecture depends on primary loading modes, requiring further mesh-convergence, cyclic, interfacial, and empirical testing.
